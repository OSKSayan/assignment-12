OLED and CRT

OLED pixels emit their own light. In a CRT, an electron beam inside a vacuum tube makes the screen's phosphor glow.

OLED

Individual pixels can switch off to produce deep blacks. OLED pixels usually switch quickly. Repeatedly displaying static elements for long periods can wear pixels unevenly and leave a permanent mark called burn-in.

CRT

An electron gun produces a beam. A deflection system moves it across the phosphor-coated screen, which glows where the electrons strike. The tube makes the monitor large and heavy.
